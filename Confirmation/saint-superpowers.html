SAINT SUPERPOWERS

REAL PEOPLE. REAL STRUGGLES. EXTRAORDINARY LOVE.

Saints weren't born perfect. They had dreams, fears, weaknesses and struggles. Discover how God worked through their lives — and what He can do through yours.

Confirmation resources

Could YOU become a saint?

You don't need to be famous, perfect, clever, beautiful or popular to become a saint. God already loves you.

Every person here had strengths and struggles. Some made serious mistakes. Others faced suffering, insecurity or difficult choices.

Their superpower wasn't magic. It was learning to cooperate with God's grace.
🍷

ST MONICA

THE NEVER-GIVE-UP HERO
SUPERPOWER: PERSEVERANCE

Who I was

I was a young girl who grew up to become the mother of St Augustine. I loved my family deeply, even when family life was difficult.

My superpowers

  • Never giving up on people I loved.
  • Praying even when nothing seemed to change.
  • Recognising my own mistakes and changing.

My struggles

  • As a girl, I secretly began drinking wine from the family supply. Little by little, it became a habit.
  • A servant confronted me harshly about my drinking. It was embarrassing, but it helped me recognise what I was doing.
  • As a mother, I worried terribly about Augustine and his choices.

My turning point

I stopped the secret drinking. Later, I learned to persevere in prayer while my son followed a path I could not control.

Your turn

  • Have you ever started a bad habit that became difficult to stop?
  • Can you accept correction when somebody tells you the truth?
  • Do you worry about somebody you love?
Being holy doesn't mean never making mistakes. It means being willing to recognise them and change.

Source: St Augustine, Confessions, Book IX, chapter 8.

ST JOHN PAUL II

THE DIGNITY HERO
SUPERPOWER: KNOWING YOUR TRUE WORTH

Who I was

My name was Karol Wojtyła. I loved sport, theatre, friendship and the outdoors. I experienced grief, war and suffering before becoming Pope.

I spent years teaching young people about love, relationships and the meaning of being human.

My superpowers

  • Showing that every human person has extraordinary dignity.
  • Teaching that our bodies are good and meaningful: we are created in God's image.
  • Explaining that genuine love means respecting people, not using them.
  • Courageously defending human dignity.

My struggles

  • I lost my mother, brother and father while still young.
  • I lived through war, oppression and the destruction of many of my plans.
  • I experienced serious physical suffering, especially later in life.
  • I lived in a world where human beings were often treated as objects rather than persons.

My turning point

My experiences, faith and philosophical studies helped shape what became known as Theology of the Body.

I wanted people to understand that the body is not something shameful or disposable. Our bodies express who we are as persons.

Your turn

  • Do you ever compare your body or appearance with people on Instagram, TikTok or other social media?
  • Have filters, edited photographs or beauty standards ever made you feel that you aren't good enough?
  • Do you sometimes feel pressure to look attractive to be liked or accepted?
  • Can you love and respect your body without needing it to look perfect?
  • Do you treat others as people to love, or sometimes as people whose approval you need?
You are not a body to be judged, a photograph to be liked, or a product to be admired. You are a person created and loved by God. Your dignity does not depend on your appearance.

Inspired by John Paul II's Theology of the Body, his catecheses on human love, and his personalist understanding of human dignity. The social media examples are modern applications of his teaching, not direct quotations.

CLARE CROCKETT

THE TRANSFORMATION HERO
SUPERPOWER: STARTING AGAIN

Who I was

I was a lively teenager from Northern Ireland. I wanted to become a famous actress and television presenter. I loved excitement and being noticed.

My superpowers

  • Passion and enthusiasm.
  • Generosity and wholehearted commitment.
  • The courage to change direction.
  • Bringing joy to other people.

My struggles

  • I wanted fame, attention and success.
  • I smoked, drank and enjoyed partying.
  • Even after encountering Christ, I struggled to leave old habits behind.
  • I found it difficult to surrender my own plans.

My turning point

At seventeen, during Holy Week in Spain, I experienced a profound encounter with Jesus. My conversion was not instantaneous: I still had choices and struggles ahead of me.

Your turn

  • Do you sometimes pretend to be someone else to fit in?
  • Do you feel pressure to be popular?
  • Have you ever promised to change but fallen back into old habits?
  • What do you think would make you truly happy?
Your mistakes are part of your story, but they don't have to write the ending.

Clare Crockett is a Servant of God, not yet a canonised saint.

ST AUGUSTINE

THE COMEBACK HERO
SUPERPOWER: CHANGING YOUR LIFE

Who I was

I was intelligent, ambitious and curious. I wanted success, pleasure, friendship and answers to life's biggest questions.

My superpowers

  • Searching honestly for truth.
  • Recognising my own contradictions.
  • Learning from mistakes.
  • Having the courage to change.

My struggles

  • As a teenager, I stole pears with my friends, not because we needed them, but because we enjoyed doing something forbidden together.
  • I pursued pleasure and struggled with sexual desire.
  • I wanted to change, but kept postponing it.
  • I struggled to accept the faith of my mother, Monica.

My turning point

After years of searching, I encountered the Christian faith in a new way. I finally accepted that I needed God's grace to become the person I wanted to be.

Your turn

  • Have you ever done something wrong just because your friends did?
  • Have you ever known what was right but chosen something else?
  • Do you sometimes think: "I'll change tomorrow"?
  • Do you believe that people can really change?
You are not stuck being the person you were yesterday.

Source: St Augustine, Confessions, especially Books II and VIII.

ST CARLO ACUTIS

THE DIGITAL DISCIPLE
SUPERPOWER: USING YOUR TALENTS FOR GOOD

Who I was

I was an ordinary teenager in many ways. I enjoyed computers, video games, football, friends and technology.

I wore jeans and trainers. I didn't need to look like someone from the Middle Ages to love Jesus.

My superpowers

  • Creativity and computing skills.
  • Love for the Eucharist.
  • Generosity towards people in need.
  • Using modern technology to share faith.

My struggles

  • I had to make choices about how to spend my time, including time spent on entertainment.
  • I lived in the same world of school, technology and friendships that teenagers recognise today.
  • I faced serious illness and died aged fifteen.

My turning point

I used my computer skills to document Eucharistic miracles and share my faith with others.

Your turn

  • How much time do you spend on your phone?
  • Do you sometimes lose hours scrolling or gaming?
  • What talents could you use to help somebody?
  • Could holiness be possible in your ordinary daily life?
You don't have to become somebody else to become a saint. Start with who you are.

Carlo was canonised in 2025. Specific personal vices are not attributed to him here without reliable historical evidence.

ST PIER GIORGIO FRASSATI

THE ADVENTURE HERO
SUPERPOWER: COURAGEOUS FRIENDSHIP

Who I was

I was a young Italian who loved mountain climbing, sport, practical jokes, adventure and friendship. I came from a wealthy family.

My superpowers

  • Joy and friendship.
  • Courage to stand against injustice.
  • Generosity towards the poor and sick.
  • Living faith through action.

My struggles

  • I struggled with some school subjects, including Latin.
  • I experienced disagreements with my parents.
  • I had to decide whether to follow social expectations or the convictions of my faith.
  • I lived with the contrast between my family's privileged circumstances and the poverty around me.

My turning point

I chose to put my faith into action, visiting and helping people in need. I did not want Christianity to be just words.

Your turn

  • Do you ever feel misunderstood by your parents?
  • Have you ever struggled at school?
  • Would you stand up for someone being excluded?
  • Can you have fun and still take your faith seriously?
Life is an adventure. The greatest adventure is learning to love.

Pier Giorgio Frassati was canonised in 2025.

ST THÉRÈSE OF LISIEUX

THE LITTLE THINGS HERO
SUPERPOWER: LOVE IN SMALL ACTIONS

Who I was

I was an affectionate, sensitive girl who loved my family. I wanted to do great things for God, but I often felt small and weak.

My superpowers

  • Trusting God.
  • Loving people in ordinary situations.
  • Perseverance and humility.
  • Discovering the value of small actions.

My struggles

  • My mother died when I was very young.
  • I was extremely sensitive and cried easily.
  • I found criticism difficult.
  • I struggled with emotional dependence and wanted to become stronger.

My turning point

At fourteen, I experienced a profound change at Christmas. I began to grow beyond my childhood sensitivity.

Later I discovered my "little way": doing ordinary things with extraordinary love.

Your turn

  • Do you take criticism personally?
  • Do you sometimes feel too sensitive?
  • Do you compare your achievements with others?
  • What small act of kindness could you do today?
You don't need to be famous or extraordinary to make a difference. Love starts with small things.

Source: St Thérèse, Story of a Soul.

BLESSED CHIARA LUCE BADANO

THE LIGHT BRINGER
SUPERPOWER: HOPE

Who I was

I was an Italian teenager who loved sport, friendship and ordinary life. I enjoyed spending time with other young people.

My superpowers

  • Hope in difficult circumstances.
  • Generosity and kindness.
  • Trust in God.
  • Concern for others even while suffering.

My struggles

  • I developed a serious illness at seventeen.
  • I faced pain and uncertainty.
  • I had to confront the loss of the future I had imagined for myself.
  • I experienced real suffering; my faith did not make it disappear.

My turning point

During my illness, I tried to respond to suffering with faith and love. My hope was not the absence of sadness or pain.

Your turn

  • Have you ever felt that life was unfair?
  • Have you experienced disappointment or loss?
  • How can you support a friend who is struggling?
  • Can hope exist even when life is difficult?
Being strong doesn't mean never feeling sad. Sometimes it means choosing love in the middle of suffering.

Chiara Luce Badano was beatified in 2010. She is Blessed, not yet a canonised saint.

WHICH SUPERPOWER DO YOU NEED?

Everyone has strengths and struggles. Choose something you recognise in your own life. You don't need to tell anybody what you chose.

Choose a statement to discover someone whose story might help you.

YOUR CHALLENGE

Choose one person from these sheets. Read their story and think about three questions:

  1. What do I have in common with this person?
  2. What strength would I like to develop?
  3. What is one small thing I could do differently this week?

Remember: Saints are not superheroes who replace Jesus. They are ordinary people who allowed God's grace to transform their lives.
